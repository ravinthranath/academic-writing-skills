# Figures, tables and equations

## Numbering and referencing

- Number tables and figures **separately** and in order of first mention: Table 1, Table 2; Figure 1, Figure 2.
- Long documents (dissertations) may use chapter numbering: Figure 3.2, Table 4.1.
- Refer to every visual by number before or near where it appears: "Figure 2 shows..." Never "the figure below" or "the following table".
- Every visual needs at least one sentence in the text that says what it shows and why it matters.
  - "As Table 3 shows, the fine-tuned model reduced the false-positive rate from 8.1% to 3.4%."
- A visual that is never referred to in the text should be removed or moved to an appendix.

## Caption placement (differs by style; follow the brief)

| Style | Tables | Figures |
|---|---|---|
| Most UK/Harvard guides, IEEE, Vancouver | Title **above** | Caption **below** |
| APA 7 | Bold number and italic title **above**; *Note.* below | Bold number and italic title **above**; *Note.* below |
| MLA 9 | Label and title above | Caption below |
| Chicago | Title above | Caption below |

Captions should be informative: "Figure 4: Validation loss for three learning rates over 50 epochs" rather than "Figure 4: Loss".

## Sources for visuals

- Own work: no citation needed.
- Changed from a source: "Adapted from Smith (2021, p. 14)." and include Smith (2021) in the reference list.
- Unchanged: "Reproduced from Smith (2021)" or "Source: Smith (2021)." Reproducing published images may also need copyright permission for publication beyond coursework.
- AI-generated images: label as such and follow the AI acknowledgement rules.

## Tables

- Column headings with units: "Time (s)", "Accuracy (%)".
- Consistent decimal places; align numbers by decimal point.
- Minimal lines (no vertical rules in most academic styles).
- Bold the best result only if the brief or field convention expects it, and say so in the note.
- Do not use a table for content better written as two sentences.

## Figures and charts

- Label both axes with quantity and unit.
- Readable fonts (no smaller than the body text minus 2 pt when printed).
- Colour-blind-safe palettes; do not rely on colour alone (use markers or line styles).
- Show error bars or intervals where relevant and state what they represent.
- Start bar-chart axes at zero unless clearly marked.
- Export at sufficient resolution (vector or at least 300 dpi for print).

## Accessibility

- Add alt text that conveys the information, not "graph": "Line chart showing validation accuracy rising from 62% to 89% over 30 epochs, plateauing after epoch 22."
- Complex figures: give a fuller description in the text or an appendix.

## Equations

- Display important equations on their own line, numbered at the right margin: (1), (2).
- Refer to them as "Equation (1)" or "Eq. (1)" consistently.
- Define every symbol at first use: "where *y* is the true label and *ŷ* the predicted probability".
- Punctuate equations as part of the sentence.
- Use the equation editor or LaTeX; do not paste images of equations.

## Code

- Short snippets in a monospaced font with a listing number ("Listing 1") if the brief allows.
- Full code in an appendix or a linked repository with commit hash.
- Code usually does not count toward the word limit; check the brief.
