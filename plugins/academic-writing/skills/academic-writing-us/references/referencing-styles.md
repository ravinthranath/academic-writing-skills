# Referencing styles

Always use the style the module, department or publisher requires, at the edition they require. The examples below show the shape of each style. Check details (et al. thresholds, punctuation, access dates) in the official manual or the university library guide.

**Golden rule: never create a reference from memory.** Every reference must be checked against a DOI, publisher page, library catalogue or database record before it is used. If a source cannot be verified, flag it as [unverified source] and do not include it in the reference list.

## Which style where

| Style | Common in | Citation type |
|---|---|---|
| Harvard (many variants; Cite Them Right is the most common UK one) | UK, Australia, many business, social science, humanities and STEM courses | Author–date |
| APA 7 | Psychology, education, social sciences, nursing; widely used in the US, Canada, Australia | Author–date |
| IEEE | Electrical engineering, computer science, AI | Numbered [1] |
| ACM | Computing (ACM venues) | Numbered or author–year (venue template) |
| MLA 9 | Literature, languages, humanities (US especially) | Author–page |
| Chicago (17th/18th edn) | History, arts (notes–bibliography); some social sciences (author–date) | Footnotes or author–date |
| Vancouver | Medicine, biomedical sciences | Numbered (1) or superscript |
| OSCOLA | UK law | Footnotes |
| Bluebook | US law | Footnotes |
| AGLC | Australian law | Footnotes |
| McGill Guide | Canadian law | Footnotes |

## Examples

**Harvard (Cite Them Right)**
- In-text: (Daun and Brings, 2023) or Daun and Brings (2023) argue... With page: (Daun and Brings, 2023, p. 112).
- Conference paper: Daun, M. and Brings, J. (2023) 'How ChatGPT will change software engineering education', *Conference on innovation and technology in computer science education*. Turku, Finland, 7–12 July. New York, NY: ACM, pp. 110–116. Available at: https://doi.org/10.1145/3587102.3588815 (Accessed: 15 May 2024).
- Journal article: Author, A.A. (Year) 'Title of article', *Journal Title*, volume(issue), pp. x–y. Available at: https://doi.org/... (Accessed: date).
- Book: Author, A.A. (Year) *Title of book*. Edition. Place: Publisher.
- Et al.: library guides commonly report Cite Them Right uses et al. for four or more authors; confirm on the Cite Them Right platform.

**APA 7**
- In-text: (Grady et al., 2019) or Grady et al. (2019). Use et al. for three or more authors from the first citation.
- Journal: Grady, J. S., Her, M., Moreno, G., Perez, C., & Yelinek, J. (2019). Emotions in storybooks: A comparison of storybooks that represent ethnic and racial groups in the United States. *Psychology of Popular Media Culture, 8*(3), 207–217. https://doi.org/10.1037/ppm0000185
- Reference list: list up to 20 authors; for 21 or more, list the first 19, an ellipsis, then the final author.
- Double-spaced, hanging indent, alphabetical.

**IEEE**
- In-text: as shown by Brown [4], [5]; several studies [2], [4]–[7]. Numbers in order of first citation.
- Conference: A. Amador-Perez and R. A. Rodriguez-Solis, "Analysis of a CPW-fed annular slot ring antenna using DOE," in *Proc. IEEE Antennas Propag. Soc. Int. Symp.*, Jul. 2006, pp. 4301–4304.
- Journal: A. Author, B. Author, and C. Author, "Title of article," *Abbrev. Journal*, vol. x, no. x, pp. xxx–xxx, Mon. Year, doi: xxx.
- Reference list: list up to six authors; for more, first author then et al.

**ACM**
- Patricia S. Abril and Robert Plant. 2007. The patent holder's dilemma: Buy, sell, or troll? *Commun. ACM* 50, 1 (Jan. 2007), 36–44. https://doi.org/10.1145/1188913.1188915

**MLA 9**
- In-text: (Smith 45).
- Works Cited: Author. "Title of Article." *Container*, vol. x, no. x, Year, pp. x–y. DOI or URL.

**Chicago (notes–bibliography)**
- Footnote: 1. Author First Last, *Title* (Place: Publisher, Year), page.
- Bibliography: Last, First. *Title*. Place: Publisher, Year.

**Vancouver**
- In-text: ...as previously reported (1,3–5).
- Reference: 1. Author AA, Author BB. Title of article. Abbrev Journal. Year;vol(issue):pages. doi:...

## Online sources, datasets, software

- Datasets: author/organisation, year, title, version, publisher/repository, DOI or URL.
- Software and libraries: name, version, author/organisation, year, URL or DOI (many projects provide a CITATION file).
- Web pages: author or organisation, year, title, URL, access date (Harvard and some others require access dates; APA 7 only for pages designed to change).

## Citing generative AI (styles disagree)

| Style | Approach |
|---|---|
| Cite Them Right Harvard | Private chat treated as personal communication. Reference: OpenAI ChatGPT (2025) *ChatGPT response to [Your Name]*, 20 October. In-text: (OpenAI ChatGPT, 2025). Some universities also require the transcript as an appendix. |
| APA 7 (2025 guidance) | Not personal communication ("there is no person communicating"). Reference: OpenAI. (2025, August 21). *Title describing the chat* [Generative AI chat]. ChatGPT. https://chatgpt.com/share/... AI cannot be an author. |
| MLA 9 | Describe the prompt as the title; tool as container; company as publisher; date; URL. |
| IEEE | No formal format; follow the module or treat as software/personal communication as instructed. |

Citing an AI tool is separate from **acknowledging** AI use. Many universities require both: a citation where AI output is used in the text, and an acknowledgement statement describing how AI was used (see integrity-and-ai-use.md). Many guides advise against quoting AI output as an authority for facts; verify facts in primary sources and cite those instead.

## Checks before submission

- Every in-text citation appears in the reference list and vice versa.
- One style, used consistently (no mixed formats).
- Names, years, titles, volume/issue/pages match the actual source.
- DOIs resolve and lead to the cited work.
- Secondary citations are marked ("cited in") per the style.
- Quotations have page numbers where the style requires them.
