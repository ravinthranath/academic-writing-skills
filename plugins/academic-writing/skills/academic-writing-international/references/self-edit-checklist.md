# Common mistakes and self-editing checklist

## Common mistakes in academic reports

- Description instead of analysis (saying what sources say without evaluating them).
- Literature review written source by source instead of by theme.
- Interpretation inside the Results section.
- Conclusion introducing new material or citations.
- Recommendations not supported by the findings.
- Figures and tables missing numbers, captions, sources, or in-text discussion.
- Mixed or inconsistent referencing styles; in-text citations missing from the list.
- Secondary citations without "cited in".
- Over-quotation instead of paraphrase and analysis.
- Unhedged causal claims ("caused" where "contributed to" is justified).
- Over-hedged claims about the writer's own measured results.
- Acronyms not expanded at first use.
- Word limit exceeded, or essential content hidden in appendices.
- Objectives, results and conclusions not aligned.
- No limitations section, or limitations written as apologies.
- AI-suggested sources not verified.
- Informal language, contractions, rhetorical questions.
- Inconsistent spelling convention (mixing British and American).
- Inconsistent terminology for the same concept.

## Proofreading passes (one focus per pass)

1. **Brief:** does the work answer the actual question and meet every requirement in the brief and rubric?
2. **Structure:** does each section do only its own job?
3. **Argument:** does every paragraph have a topic sentence, evidence, analysis and a link back?
4. **Alignment:** do objectives, methods, results and conclusions map one to one?
5. **Evidence:** is every claim cited, and hedged to the right strength?
6. **Figures and tables:** numbered, captioned, referred to in the text, sources credited, alt text added?
7. **References:** every citation listed and vice versa, one style, details and DOIs checked?
8. **Language:** no contractions, consistent spelling, acronyms expanded, no informal words, no AI-tell clusters, consistent terms?
9. **Format:** word count, heading numbering, page numbers, title page, declaration, file name and format?
10. **Read aloud** (or use text-to-speech) to catch rhythm problems and missing words.

## Output report template (edit mode)

After an edit, report briefly:

- Main changes, grouped by rule (e.g. "Removed 6 contractions; replaced 4 'I think' constructions; expanded LLM on first use; numbered and captioned 2 figures; replaced 'delves into the intricate interplay' with a concrete claim").
- Flags for the writer: [citation needed], [figure needed], [unverified source], [check brief].
- Word count before and after.
- Any content-level problems that need the writer's own input (missing analysis, weak evidence, misaligned conclusion).
- One-line AI-use reminder.
